An article is the finished form. It makes an argument, it is meant to still be
true in a year, and it earns the serif column and the reading time estimate at
the top. If a piece is not ready to be defended, it is a build log.

## Why not just call it a blog

"Blog" is a container, not a promise. It lets a half-formed thought inherit the
authority of a finished piece, and it gives a reader no way to calibrate how
much weight to put on what they are reading.

Three named types fix that, because each one sets a different expectation:

- **Article** — argued and finished. Serif body, full reading treatment.
- **Build log** — dated and provisional. Sans body, tighter rhythm, written while the thing was happening.
- **Note** — one idea, short, no preamble.

The type is set in frontmatter and shows up three times: as a label on the
index, as a coloured left rule beside it, and as the eyebrow on the piece
itself. The label is the real signal — the colour is a shortcut for people who
can use it, never the only cue.

## What belongs in an article

Something a reader can act on, with the reasoning attached. If the piece is
mostly numbers, it is probably an experiment and belongs in the Lab, with a
short article pointing at it.

Cross-link the two with the `experiment` field in frontmatter, and a callout to
the measurements appears at the foot of the article automatically.
